# Travel Preferences & Trip-Guide Spec

**Purpose.** Hand this file to Claude at the start of planning any new destination. It captures who we are, how we travel, and the exact shape of the document we want back. Start a new session with the prompt at the bottom.

**Last updated:** August 2026 (built while planning Québec & Montréal, Aug 26 – Sep 6, 2026 — see `destinations/montreal-quebec/` — its `guide.html` and `PLANNING-NOTES.md` are the worked example of the finished deliverable)

---

## 1. Who's travelling

- **Two adults, married.** **A** (non-vegetarian) and **K** (**vegetarian**).
- Home base: **Cheshire, Connecticut**. Most trips start and end in our own driveway.
- **We drive** on trips within reach of Connecticut. For anything we fly to, see §4 — public transport first, a rental car only where it earns its keep.
- Comfortable with **hard physical days** — a 9-mile hike with 4,000 ft of gain is a good day, not a problem.
- We work. Trips usually start **after work (~6 p.m.)** on the departure day. A Wednesday-evening head-start drive is often worth more than an extra day off.

---

## 2. What makes a trip great for us

Ranked. A destination earns its place by hitting several of these, not one.

1. **History** — deep, specific, place-anchored. Not "this is old" but *what happened here, who decided what, and why here*.
2. **Spectacular nature** — alpine ridges, gorges, waterfalls, coastlines, wildlife. We hike.
3. **Spectacular architecture** — great buildings, named, dated, and explained. Interiors count as much as skylines.
4. **Unique food** — regional, specific, ideally impossible to get at home. Markets and producers over restaurant lists.
5. **Unique cultural events** — festivals, ceremonies, live music, night-walks, anything that only happens *while we're there*.
6. **The genuine must-sees** — we don't want to skip the famous thing out of contrarianism. Hit the icons *and* the hidden gems.

**The unifying test:** would this be a story we tell for years?

---

## 3. How we want trips structured

- **Hub and spoke, always.** Stay in one base and day-trip out. Move bases only when the driving math genuinely demands it — and when a plan proposes a move, it should say what it's buying in daylight hours.
- **Minimize repacking.** Four bases over twelve days is fine. Eight is not.
- **Days can be jam-packed.** Don't pad the itinerary to be gentle. Give us more than we can do and tell us what to cut, in order.
- **The car disappears in cities.** Park once, walk and use transit. Don't build city days that need driving.
- **Protect the marquee days.** Weather-dependent days (big hikes, boat trips) get a built-in backup day and an explicit go/no-go rule.

---

## 4. Getting there and getting around

**Domestic / regional trips.** We drive our own car from the driveway in Cheshire, CT. Road distance is a hard constraint — treat drive time as part of the itinerary, not an afterthought. Leaving after work (~6 p.m.) the night before is often worth more than a day off.

**International trips.** We fly. Assume arrival at a major hub airport, and plan the first and last days around flight times and jet lag.

**Default to public transport.** In cities we want to walk and use metro, bus and train. Never build a city day that requires a car — parking, city driving and one-way streets are pure cost. If a city has a good regional rail network, check whether it reaches the day trips before assuming a car is needed.

**Rent a car only where it genuinely buys time.** Mountains, national parks, coastal drives, rural villages, wine and food regions — anywhere transit is sparse or would cost hours of waiting. Be explicit about *which segments need a car and which don't*, rather than defaulting to a car for the whole trip.

**Preferred pattern: rent for a defined block in the middle.** Fly in → transit in city A → pick up a car for the rural leg → drop it back → transit in city B → fly out. This is usually both cheaper and less stressful than holding a car for the full trip.

### One-way rentals — always price them, never assume

Picking up and dropping off at **different locations** carries a real premium. It ranges from trivial to enormous depending on country, operator and distance, and cross-border one-ways are often the worst of all.

- **Look up the actual quoted price for both options** — same-location loop vs one-way — and **show the delta** before recommending either.
- Weigh the premium against the hours saved. If a one-way costs modestly and saves half a day of backtracking, take it. If it doubles the rental cost, plan a loop.
- **Default is same-location pickup and drop-off** unless the one-way premium has been quantified and justified.

Also check, every time:

- Cross-border restrictions — many European rentals forbid or surcharge taking the car into certain countries
- Minimum rental periods and whether a shorter block is even offered
- Automatic vs manual availability and the automatic surcharge (often significant outside North America)
- International Driving Permit requirements
- Tolls, vignettes, congestion and low-emission zones, and how they're paid
- Fuel policy, young/senior driver surcharges, additional-driver fees, insurance/CDW
- **Whether a rail pass or point-to-point tickets simply beat driving** for that country — sometimes the honest answer is don't rent at all

## 5. Lodging

- **Walking distance to everything** in cities. Non-negotiable.
- **Authentic residential neighbourhoods, not tourist zones.** We want the street locals live on — real cafés, real grocery stores, real prices — with a 10-minute walk to the monuments. Explicitly steer us *away* from the pretty historic core if that's where the tourist trap is.
- **Budget: ~US$100/night target.** Up to **US$150–200 if it's genuinely amazing**. Above ~$150, tell us honestly whether a hotel beats an apartment at that price in that city — often it does.
- **Airbnb and hotels both fine.** Give **live search links with our dates pre-filled**, plus 2–4 named specific properties per base with rough prices.
- **Parking must be confirmed** in writing before booking, in any city.
- Flag **local short-term-rental legality** (registration numbers, permits) where it matters.

---

## 6. Food — this is where most guides fail us

Always split recommendations into **three tagged tiers**:

- **FULLY VEG** — entirely vegetarian/vegan places. K's destination meals.
- **BOTH** — genuinely excellent for K *and* serious food for A, at the same table. **This tier matters most — maximize it.**
- **ANAND ONLY** — famous non-veg institutions worth doing, flagged clearly so we plan around them (takeaway, split up, or skip).

Also always include:

- **Grocery stores**, named, with the cheap ones identified. We eat breakfast at home and graze lunch from markets; one good dinner out most nights. This saves real money and suits a vegetarian better than three restaurant meals a day.
- **Markets and producers** — these are attractions, not errands.
- **Local-language phrases for dietary needs**, and a warning about regional cuisines where fish / bacon / meat stock are not locally considered "meat".
- Rough price bands and which places need booking how far ahead.

---

## 7. Tours & guides

For every city, in this order:

1. **Greeters** — free local-volunteer walks (`internationalgreeter.org`). Check whether the city has a programme; they're the best version of this and need 2–4 weeks' notice. Say plainly if a city has none.
2. **Free / pay-what-you-wish walking tours** — with booking lead time and a fair tip figure.
3. **Self-guided audio tours** — VoiceMap and equivalents, with prices and a reminder to download offline.
4. **Free official guided programmes** — national park services, historic sites, museums.
5. One or two **paid tours worth the money**, and why.

Put a guided walk on the **first full day** in each city, not the last.

---

## 8. Maps — required, and specific

- **One overall route map** showing city-to-city, with a leg table: distance, driving time, and notes per leg.
- **One detail map per region/city** showing the relative position of every attraction, the base, and the transit lines.
- Maps should be **inline SVG drawn from real coordinates** so they always render and never depend on an API key or network. Say when they're schematic.
- Pair maps with **live navigation links**; never expect the map to be used for actual wayfinding.

---

## 9. Images

- **Every image must work.** Verify each URL resolves before shipping.
- Prefer **Wikimedia Commons** (stable, hotlink-permitted) and **download local copies** into an `images/` folder next to the HTML, with a remote fallback wired up in JS.
- Images must be **representative** — the actual building, trail or town named in that section, not a generic stock shot of the region.

---

## 10. Voice & writing

- **Write like a luxury tour operator** — Tauck, Abercrombie & Kent — then add the logistics they'd leave out.
- **Powerful and to the point.** Lead each attraction with two evocative sentences that say *why it's extraordinary*, concretely.
- **Then all the detail.** Named buildings, named trails, dates, dimensions, architects, distances, elevation gain. Specifics over adjectives.
- **Checkbox lists of reasons.** For each major sight, a tick list of every superlative and reason it's a must-see — the things you'd tell someone to convince them. Superlatives must be true and verifiable ("only X north of Y", "taller than Niagara", "UNESCO since 1985").
- **No cheesy filler, no redundancy, no hedging.** Cut anything that could apply to any destination.
- **Keep rationale out of the guide.** Trade-offs, alternatives considered, and comparisons to commercial tours go in a **separate planning-notes document**.

---

## 11. Document structure — the deliverable

A **single self-contained HTML file** (plus an `images/` folder), openable offline, printable, readable on a phone.

**Three-layer progressive disclosure, using `<details>`/`<summary>`:**

- **Layer 1 — always visible.** Day/city header + a numbered "at a glance" list of that day's attractions with time and cost. Scannable in five seconds.
- **Layer 2 — one click.** Per attraction: photo, the punchy two-sentence pitch, meta pills (duration, price, booking needed), and the practical logistics.
- **Layer 3 — second click.** The full detail: what you actually walk through (named), and the complete checkbox list of reasons it's a must-see.

Plus an **Expand all / Collapse all** control per day, deep-link support, and auto-expand when printing.

**Sections to include:**

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| Hero | Theme-driven headline, dates, one-line pitch |
| The thread | The *argument* of the trip — how it connects to the region's history/culture, and how each day answers it |
| Why go | Every superlative, UNESCO listing, one-of-a-kind, and time-limited event, as tick lists |
| The route | Overall map + leg table + base summary |
| Where to stay | Per base: neighbourhood rationale, price reality, named options, **groceries**, parking |
| Tours | Greeters, free walks, audio, official programmes |
| Day by day | The three-layer itinerary, with region maps |
| Food | The three-tier vegetarian split, markets, groceries, phrases |
| Events calendar | **Every night accounted for**, free things called out. Not a footnote. |
| Hidden gems | The things nobody tells you, already folded into the days |
| Museums | What's in, what's missing, honest ranking |
| Watch/read/listen first | Films, TV, books, music to prime the trip |
| Gear | Weather table, hiking kit, city kit, documents/money/phone |
| Book & budget | Booking timeline in three urgency tiers, price table, total estimate |
| Driving | Local rules that differ, signage, parking, border, live-check links |
| Sources | Every official operator link, with a "verify before you pay" caveat |

**Also always deliver:** a separate `PLANNING-NOTES.md` with the reasoning, trade-offs, what was cut and why, and comparison to commercial tour operators covering the same ground.

## 12. Standing research checklist

For any new destination, always check:

- **Free/discounted admission windows** (e.g. Canada Strong Pass, national free-museum days) — these can be worth hundreds.
- **Festivals and events during the exact dates**, especially free outdoor music. Put them in the itinerary, on the day.
- **Seasonal cutoffs** — ceremonies, boat schedules, shuttles, trail cut-off times, attractions that close the week we arrive. Say plainly when we *miss* something.
- **Weekly closures** — museums shut Mondays, Sunday opening hours for churches.
- **Peak-season / holiday-weekend lodging pressure** on our specific dates.
- **What serious travellers say** — Rick Steves forums and similar, for what's overrated and what's worth more days.
- **Local short-term-rental law**, tipping norms, driving rules, border requirements.
- **Transport economics**: rail vs driving for that country; **actual quoted car-rental prices for both a same-location loop and a one-way**, with the delta stated; airport-to-city transit options; multi-day transit passes.

---

## 13. Things we don't want

- Filler attractions to pad a day. If a stop is weak, cut it and say so.
- Generic descriptions that could apply anywhere.
- Itineraries that move us every night.
- Restaurant lists that assume everyone eats meat, or that treat "we have a salad" as a vegetarian option.
- A pretty document we can't actually plan a trip from. **Addresses, hours, prices, booking links, parking, and grocery stores or it isn't finished.**

## 14. Prompt to start a new trip

> I want to plan a trip to **[DESTINATION]**, **[DATES]** — driving from Cheshire, CT, or flying into **[AIRPORT]**.
>
> Read `shared/TRAVEL-PREFERENCES.md` first — it has who we are, how we travel, and the exact spec for the document I want. `tools/README.md` covers the map machinery, and `CLAUDE.md` the repo rules.
>
> Non-negotiables: hub-and-spoke bases, walkable authentic neighbourhoods, the vegetarian/omnivore food split, verified working images, real maps on a real basemap, every evening accounted for, and the three-layer expandable HTML structure.
>
> If we're flying: default to public transport, and tell me exactly which segments actually need a rental car. If a rental is needed, **price both a same-location loop and a one-way pickup/drop-off and show me the difference** — don't assume one-way is fine.
>
> Research current facts on the web before writing — hours, prices, festivals during our exact dates, seasonal cutoffs and closures. Tell me what we'd be missing and what would make it once-in-a-lifetime.
>
> Deliver it as a new folder `destinations/<slug>/`: `guide.html`, `images/`, `meta.json`, `maps/` (with `maps.json` and `markers.py`), and `PLANNING-NOTES.md` with your reasoning and trade-offs.

## 15. The toolchain (for whoever builds the next one)

The Québec guide is not a one-off document — it is the output of a small
pipeline that lives in **`tools/`** in this repo. Read **`tools/README.md`**
before building a guide for a new city; it covers the whole thing end to end.

Each trip lives in its own folder under `destinations/<slug>/`, and every tool
takes `--dest <slug>`. A landing page listing all of them is generated from
each folder's `meta.json`. See `README.md` for the layout and `CLAUDE.md` for
the rules.

What is already built and reusable:

- **`tiles.py`** — downloads Esri World Topo basemap tiles for any bounding box.
  Real terrain, real landmarks, no API key, works offline once downloaded.
- **`resolve.py`** — resolves every map marker's coordinates from OpenStreetMap
  and Wikidata, and records the source and the date. **Coordinates are never
  typed by hand.** The first version of the Québec maps was, and three markers
  were 300–420 m out.
- **`overlay.py`** — projects lat/lon onto the tiles and draws routes, markers
  and labels as inline SVG.
- **`boxes.py`** — refuses to let two labels overlap or a label cover another
  marker's dot.
- **`maps.py` / `validate.py`** — splice into the guide and check nothing broke.
- **`build.py`** (repo root) — inject the hosted-only `<head>` tags from
  `meta.json`, verify every asset resolves, render the landing page, write
  `dist/`.
- **`check.sh`** (repo root) — the whole chain as one gate, and what CI runs.
- **`deploy.sh`** (repo root) — check, build, commit, push. GitHub Actions
  publishes to Pages from there.

The guide's own structure — six parts, collapsible days, thumbnail maps that
expand — is worth copying too. See `PLANNING-NOTES.md` §8 for why it is shaped
that way.
